Game Development Paths:



Game Design: 

Simply put, Game Design is the process of creating the rules or concepts for a game. 
While commonly associated with video games this applies to board games, role playing games and any game as a whole. 
Game design is what allows a game to flow a certain way to achieve a certain purpose. 
For example a game centered around telling a story will have mechanics in place to ensure that story is told. 
Whether that be limiting a players freedom to some degree or allowing more freedom to emphasize exploration.

Game Programming: 

Game programming is the art of using code and technology to bring concepts to life in the form of games. 
This being one of the core aspects of Game Development means it has some of the most depth in its field and programming varies heavily depending on the game. 
There are a variety of ways to program a game, the amount of languages alone makes it essentially impossible to know everything about this area of game development but that is what makes it interesting. 
It requires the programmer to think outside the box to solve or come up with ways to make a specific vision for a game. 

Game Artist: 

A game artist works on the art for a game, in reference to video games specifically they create the models, sprites, textures, artwork and anything else visually related to the game. 
Because of how unique games and art are as a whole there are a wide variety of game artists that all do different things. 
3D and 2D artists have to figure out how to best implement their craft in order to fit the needs of their game, allowing for unique pieces of work that work in tandem with game programmers and designers.

Narrative Designer: 

Narrative Designers create the story for a game, creating a story that flows well with how the game plays and feels, quests, and dialogue to immerse players into the games world. 
Narrative Designers also need to be able to work and communicate well with others and be well versed in Game Development as a whole in order to ensure the game's visions come true.

FX Artist:


FX Artists simulate effects and phenomena (whether that be based upon real life or fictional), by researching how those interact and creating multiple concepts. 
As stated previously research is a big portion of this job as needing to simulate something realistically, such as lightning, would require a good understanding of how that thing works and then figuring out how to implement it into a game.






Game Programming Disciplines:

Game Engines: 

Engine programmers create the game engines needed to run games, finding ways to optimize engines is crucial and this field relies heavily on mathematics to help create the engine. 
Communication is also integral as the engine must be created to help fuel a specific idea and taking feedback from others helps greatly.

Graphics:

Graphics programmers come up with ways to make the game look as good as possible. 
Using math and communicating with their team to achieve their preferred graphics without impacting gameplay.

AI:

This position can vary in range from what they actually work on within the game but they commonly work with NPC’s to give these characters the illusion that they are “real” entities capable of thoughts and emotions. 
Math is important yet again but problem solving and logic skills are also essential for this field as well as understanding how characters would or are supposed to behave, so some psychology experience is also needed.

UI:

UI programmers create the User Interface for players to work with so they can better understand and enjoy the game. 
While the specifics of this job vary depending on the type of game being created, UI programmers can use coding languages such as C++ and even sometimes UI specific languages such as XML.

Tools:

Game Tools Programmers create the tools used by other game devs in order to allow them to effectively do their job. 
Interpersonal skills are key here as you are getting feedback from other games devs and you need to be able to understand what they need in order to create the game. 
They also work heavily with object oriented coding and that allows for a lot of skills to be transferable between languages.
